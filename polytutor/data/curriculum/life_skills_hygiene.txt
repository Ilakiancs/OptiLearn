Staying Healthy with Good Hygiene

Hygiene means keeping yourself clean and healthy. Good hygiene habits protect you from illnesses caused by germs. Germs are tiny living things that we cannot see, but they can make us very sick if they get into our bodies. Practising good hygiene every day is one of the most important things you can do to stay healthy.

Why Hygiene Matters

Our hands touch many things every day: soil, animals, food, and other people. Germs from these surfaces can transfer to our hands and then into our mouths, eyes, and noses when we touch our faces. Washing hands properly removes germs and breaks the chain of infection.

Diseases like diarrhoea, cholera, and common colds spread easily when people do not wash their hands. Regular handwashing can prevent many of these illnesses and keep you and your family healthier.

How to Wash Your Hands Properly

Washing hands correctly takes about 20 seconds, which is roughly as long as singing a simple song twice.

Step 1: Wet your hands with clean water. Turn on the tap or pour water over your hands.

Step 2: Apply soap. Rub the soap to create lather. If you have no soap, use ash or sand as a substitute.

Step 3: Scrub all parts of your hands. Rub your palms together. Scrub the back of each hand. Clean between all your fingers. Rub around your thumbs. Scrub under your fingernails by rubbing them against your palm.

Step 4: Keep scrubbing for at least 20 seconds.

Step 5: Rinse your hands under clean running water until all the soap is gone.

Step 6: Dry your hands with a clean cloth or let them air dry. Avoid wiping your hands on your clothes if they are not clean.

When to Wash Your Hands

There are key moments when handwashing is especially important. Always wash your hands before eating food, before preparing or cooking food, after using the toilet or latrine, after blowing your nose or coughing, after touching animals or animal waste, and after handling rubbish.

Washing hands at these key moments stops the most common ways that germs spread.

Daily Hygiene Routine

A good daily hygiene routine includes more than just handwashing. In the morning, wash your face with clean water to remove sleep and dust. Brush your teeth twice a day, in the morning and before bedtime, to prevent tooth decay. Change and wash your clothes regularly. Bathe or wash your body each day if water is available.

Keep your living area clean. Sweep floors, dispose of rubbish properly, and store food in covered containers to keep insects and animals away.

Hygiene at School

At school, keep your desk clean. Use a clean handkerchief or tissue when you sneeze or cough. Share equipment that is wiped clean. Drink from a clean, covered water container. Remind friends to wash their hands before eating.

Teaching good hygiene habits to everyone around you makes the whole community healthier. When one person practises hygiene, they help protect everyone nearby.
