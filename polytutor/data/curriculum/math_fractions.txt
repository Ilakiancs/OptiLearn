What Are Fractions?

A fraction is a way of writing a number that is less than a whole. When we break something into equal parts and take some of those parts, we use a fraction to describe how many parts we have.

Every fraction has two numbers. The number on the top is called the numerator. The numerator tells us how many parts we have taken. The number on the bottom is called the denominator. The denominator tells us how many equal parts the whole was divided into.

For example, if you cut a pizza into 4 equal slices and eat 1 slice, you have eaten one out of four pieces. We write this as the fraction 1/4. The 1 on top is the numerator and the 4 on the bottom is the denominator.

Let us look at another example. If you have an apple and cut it into 2 equal pieces and give one piece to your friend, each of you has half the apple. We write half as 1/2. The numerator is 1 and the denominator is 2.

Here are some more examples of fractions. If a chocolate bar is broken into 8 equal pieces and you eat 3 pieces, you have eaten 3/8 of the chocolate. If a jug of water is divided into 5 equal cups and you drink 2 cups, you have drunk 2/5 of the jug.

Comparing Fractions

When two fractions have the same denominator, the fraction with the bigger numerator is the larger fraction. For example, 3/5 is greater than 2/5 because 3 is more than 2.

When fractions have the same numerator but different denominators, the fraction with the smaller denominator is actually larger. For example, 1/2 is greater than 1/4 because halves are bigger pieces than quarters.

Equivalent Fractions

Sometimes two different fractions describe the same amount. These are called equivalent fractions. For example, 1/2 is the same as 2/4 and 4/8. They all describe exactly half of something.

You can find equivalent fractions by multiplying or dividing both the numerator and denominator by the same number. For example, multiply 1/2 by 3 on top and 3 on bottom to get 3/6, which still equals one half.

Fractions in Real Life

Fractions appear everywhere in daily life. When we share food equally among friends, we are using fractions. When we tell time, a half hour is 30 minutes because the hour is split into 60 equal minutes and we take 30. Recipes use fractions such as 1/4 cup of sugar. Measurements use fractions such as 3/4 of a metre.

Understanding fractions helps us share things fairly and measure accurately. Practice recognising fractions in everyday situations and you will find them very natural to use.
