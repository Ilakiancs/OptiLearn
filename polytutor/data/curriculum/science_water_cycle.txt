The Water Cycle

Water is always moving. It travels from the oceans and rivers up into the sky and then falls back down to the ground. This continuous journey of water is called the water cycle. The water cycle has no beginning and no end. It repeats again and again, keeping our planet supplied with fresh water.

The water cycle has four main stages: evaporation, condensation, precipitation, and collection. Let us look at each stage carefully.

Evaporation

Evaporation is the first stage. When the sun shines on water in oceans, rivers, lakes, and puddles, it warms the water. As the water gets warm enough, tiny particles of water turn from liquid into invisible water vapour, which is a gas. This gas rises up into the air. You cannot see water vapour with your eyes, but it is all around us.

Think of a wet handkerchief left in the sun. After some time it becomes dry because the water has evaporated. The water has turned into vapour and gone into the air.

Condensation

As water vapour rises higher into the sky, the air gets cooler. When water vapour cools down, it turns back into tiny droplets of liquid water. This process is called condensation. These millions of tiny water droplets come together to form clouds. Clouds are made entirely of these tiny droplets hanging in the air.

You can see condensation at home. When you breathe on a cold glass or mirror, you see drops of water appear. That is condensation happening because your warm breath meets the cold surface.

Precipitation

When clouds collect more and more water droplets, the droplets become too heavy to stay in the air. They fall back to the ground. This falling water is called precipitation. Precipitation can take different forms depending on the temperature of the air. When it is warm, precipitation falls as rain. When the air is very cold, precipitation falls as snow, sleet, or hail.

Rain is the most common form of precipitation in warm countries. It fills rivers, lakes, and ponds. It soaks into the soil and provides water for plants and animals.

Collection

After precipitation falls, water collects in rivers, lakes, oceans, and underground. Some water soaks into the ground and becomes groundwater that can be reached by wells. Rivers carry water from mountains down to the sea. Plants absorb some water through their roots.

Once water is collected in oceans and rivers, the sun begins to warm it again and the cycle starts over with evaporation.

Why the Water Cycle Matters

The water cycle is essential for all life on Earth. It brings fresh water to land, supports plant growth, fills rivers for drinking and farming, and controls the weather. Without the water cycle, rain would stop and the land would dry out. Understanding the water cycle helps us appreciate the importance of protecting rivers, lakes, and forests.
