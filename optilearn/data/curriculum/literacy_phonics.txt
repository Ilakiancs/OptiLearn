Learning to Read with Phonics

Phonics is a method of learning to read by connecting letters to the sounds they make. Every letter in the alphabet has at least one sound. When we learn to match letters to sounds, we can begin to read simple words.

The English alphabet has 26 letters. Some letters make one clear sound. For example, the letter B makes the sound at the beginning of the word "ball." The letter M makes the sound at the beginning of "mango." The letter S makes the sound at the beginning of "sun."

Vowels and Consonants

Letters are divided into two groups: vowels and consonants. The vowels are A, E, I, O, and U. All other letters are consonants. Vowels are special because they are found in almost every word, and every syllable must contain a vowel sound.

The short vowel sounds are very important for beginners. A makes the short sound in "cat." E makes the short sound in "bed." I makes the short sound in "sit." O makes the short sound in "hot." U makes the short sound in "cup."

CVC Words

A CVC word is a word made of three parts: a Consonant, a Vowel, and a Consonant. CVC words are the first words most children learn to read because they are short and follow clear sound patterns.

Here are some examples of CVC words. The word "cat" has the consonant C, the vowel A, and the consonant T. Say each sound one at a time: kuh, aah, tuh. Now blend them together quickly: cat.

The word "pin" has the consonant P, the vowel I, and the consonant N. The sounds are: puh, ih, nuh. Blended together: pin.

The word "dog" has D, O, and G. The sounds are: duh, oh, guh. Together: dog.

Other common CVC words include: bed, cup, hat, red, big, top, sun, map, leg, and hut.

Blending Sounds

Blending means joining sounds together smoothly to read a word. First, say each sound separately, keeping a slight pause between sounds. Then say the sounds closer together until they flow as one word.

Practice blending each day. Start with two sounds and build up to three. For example, the word "at" has two sounds: aah and tuh. Blend them to read "at." Then add a consonant at the start to make a CVC word: "bat," "cat," "hat," "mat," "rat," "sat."

Segmenting Sounds

Segmenting is the opposite of blending. When we segment a word, we break it into its individual sounds. For example, the word "cup" has three sounds: kuh, uh, puh. Segmenting helps with spelling because we can write down each sound we hear.

Reading Practice Tips

Point to each letter when you sound it out. Say the sounds slowly and then blend them faster and faster until the word sounds natural. Do not guess from the pictures alone. Trust the letter sounds. Read simple CVC words every day in short sessions of ten to fifteen minutes. With daily practice, reading becomes faster and more enjoyable.
